### Meeting privacy and publicity

*Summary:* Discussed how open the team's meetings should be, given that they routinely cover people and money.

* Team meetings are private by default, based on the content discussed.
* Office hours will be open and public.
* Specific meetings may include invited advisors or guests.
* An "advisors" channel will be set up so people who want to cooperate with the team have an avenue to do so, without opening the primary meeting.
* Resolutions will be posted for comment before being finalized, and we'll keep people updated on the state of our discussions.

### Office hours

We'll publish best-effort time slots and adjust based on who can flex to attend, since there's no perfect overlap.

### Decision axioms

We adopted the following axioms for deciding what to fund:

* **End-user impact first.** Prefer to fund a Maintainer in Residence to maintain areas of the project that have (direct or indirect) impact on Rust users' experience.
* **Dire needs first.** Address the teams with the greatest need first — raise the floor before raising the ceiling.
* **Fund what no one else will.** Prefer maintenance, review, and triage — work with no natural corporate sponsor and no realistic path to directed funding.
* **Scale to demand.** For areas with lots of potential for directed funding, still fund maintainers to maintain the code between feature development.
* **Shovel-ready.** Between equal-priority needs, fund the one that can start soonest.

### Funding rate

Flat rates for a given amount of funded activity, with small adjustments for individual circumstances:

* ~$10K USD / month for full time
* ~$5K USD / month for half time
* ~$2K USD / day
* ~$1K USD / half-day

These may be adjusted as we gain experience. They are data-driven, based on what applicants requested and on the rate paid by the Zig foundation.

### Severance

*Summary:* We want a Maintainer in Residence to have a cushion even if we're unable to renew their contract.

* MiR contracts are for one year. We'd like to reserve a buffer (an "off-ramp") so that, if a contract cannot be renewed due to inadequate funding, the person has some months of runway to plan their transition.
* Discussion favored framing this as a "buffer" rather than "severance," and applying it to full-time contracts in particular (the transition is less acute for those funded at small fractions).
* We'd want to notify a maintainer well ahead of a non-renewal (contracts otherwise run for the full year, barring termination for cause).
* The best way to budget for this to be raised with the Foundation.

Whether and when a long-tenured maintainer might transition to being a Foundation employee was raised but set aside as out of scope for now.

### Payment logistics

Some prospective maintainers may, for legal reasons, need payment routed through an intermediary (an umbrella company) rather than paid directly. We'd like the flexibility to support people in these situations. This, along with how to account for the buffer above, will be raised with the Foundation.

### Review shortlist and plan

*Summary:* Walked through a shortlist of candidates by team and agreed to begin reaching out.

* The shortlist spans several of the most important teams. Notably it does not include compiler or libs — the compiler already has substantial other funding, and some libs work can be covered by a candidate slotted elsewhere.
* **rustfmt:** no one has come forward through the form despite outreach, so it drops off this round.
* **rust-analyzer:** no one is looking for funding at the moment.
* **Moderation:** rather than a single MiR, there's more appetite for moderation-team training so the team has shared standards, accountability, and resources for its decisions. The plan is to approach the team, note we can offer some funding, and work out together how best to spend it.

### Dedicated funding

We'll allow dedicated funding earmarked for particular individuals or teams (e.g. Clippy, rustfmt, cargo), provided the recipient operates like any other Maintainer in Residence.
