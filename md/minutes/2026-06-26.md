# Meeting 2026-06-26

### Agenda

* Discussion with Predrag
* Review proposals for how to spend money

### Discussion with Predrag

*Summary:* Predrag joined to talk through how to set the fund up for long-term success — the emphasis was on mentorship, making progress legible to sponsors, and choosing marketable milestones.

Discussion covered:

* A number of corporate sponsors are expected to be featured in the initial funding announcement.
* Some high-value targets (e.g. rustfmt, Clippy) have no associated project goal and no one has yet applied through the funding form. Preference is to go "through the front door" — encourage the relevant maintainers to apply rather than route around the process. Options include nudging team members who haven't applied to submit, asking an existing or prospective Maintainer-in-Residence to also cover an area, or holding funds and inviting an application.
* **Mentorship.** Mentorship is a core expectation of MiR-style funding: recruiting, building up review capacity, and taking on long-neglected "hard" PRs. In team-health conversations, several team leads reported a backlog of difficult work that no one has time to give attention to; the hope is that funded time frees people up to mentor and to tackle those.
* **Making work legible.** Sponsors need to see observable progress to justify continued funding. We should help fundees structure their work into meaningful milestones — this is a skill, not everyone has it — rather than requiring polished blog posts. Merged PRs and shipped changes are enough to demonstrate progress; "we did some stuff but there's no observable progress toward the goal" is not.
* Lightweight milestone tracking (e.g. a simple checkbox spreadsheet), possibly with a program manager helping keep it current, was floated.
* **Framing.** Help fundees succeed ("we'll help you structure this so it works") rather than imposing requirements ("do this or you don't get paid"). Successful goals lead to more money.
* **Project metrics.** Several approaches were discussed for measuring maintenance impact and review bus-factor: Octopodi (an existing tool focused on review bus-factor), Jakub's work on analyzing individual activity and interactions with the project, and the CHAOSS metrics framework (via the Linux Foundation). Consensus: standing up CHAOSS is a low bar and worth doing, but getting started should not be blocked on analytics, nor should heavier analytics be blocked on CHAOSS. Skepticism was expressed about larger platforms that promise much but show little in practice.
* Maintenance work is easiest to quantify via before/after numbers (e.g. open PR / bug counts) plus changelogs; short write-ups by people who landed work thanks to funded maintenance also help.
* **Fungibility.** If the fund helps get the goals a sponsor cares about funded, the sponsor may not mind whether the money flows through the fund directly. Any such arrangement needs careful framing so it doesn't read as "I paid into the fund to get my pet goal done."

*Sizing:* Rough consensus to fund a modest number of people (on the order of a handful, not more than ~10), mixing one or more larger (roughly full-time) commitments with several smaller slices. A preference was expressed for fewer people with larger slices — the biggest risk is spreading money so thin that many things make a little progress but nothing actually gets *done*.

### Review proposals for how to spend money

*Summary:* Reviewed proposals from two team members for allocating the fund. Both start from the same principle: **decide team needs first, independent of which specific individuals might be funded.** The proposals differed mainly in priority ordering and in their assumptions about which people would be funded through other channels.

Prioritization axioms:

1. **Security and critical infrastructure first.** Prioritize teams where an unfunded gap creates supply-chain or safety risk.
2. **Dire needs.** Address the teams with the greatest need first — raise the floor before raising the ceiling.
3. **Fund what no one else will.** Prefer maintenance, review, and triage — work with no natural corporate sponsor and no realistic path to directed funding.
4. **Scale to demand.** For areas with lots of potential for directed funding, still fund maintainers to keep the code maintained between feature development.
5. **Shovel-ready.** Between equal-priority needs, fund the one that can start soonest.

Applying the axioms to teams (illustrative):

* *rustup* — critical infrastructure and dire need (no current funding).
* *cargo* — critical infrastructure and dire need; also more amenable to directed funding, so ranked slightly lower.
* *moderation* — dire need, and something only the fund would cover.
* *Clippy* — dire need (no current funding), though not exclusively something the fund must cover (could potentially be supplemented from elsewhere).
* *rustfmt* — dire need, but **not shovel-ready** — we should flag this clearly.
* *libs, rustdoc, rust-analyzer* — dire need.
* *infra* — something only the fund would cover.
* *compiler* — scale-to-demand.

A rough, tentative sketch of effort had cargo, rustup, and rust-analyzer around full-time, with Clippy, moderation, and rustfmt around half-time.

**Moderation.** There is a recognized need for a shared, coherent vision of what moderation in the project should look like; recent discussion left some moderators re-invigorated but highlighted a lack of a common understanding of expectations. Funding could be applied flexibly here — for example toward training or an offsite to get the team on the same page — provided we're confident it's used well.

**Gathering more data.** For some candidates we want to gather more information before committing, particularly in security-sensitive areas and where an individual has a shorter history in the project. We also want to understand what support individuals need (including whether the Foundation can help provide it).

**Renewal and severance.** We should not assume existing external funding will automatically renew, and should make the case to key funders about the project's continued critical status. There was also interest in building a form of "severance" into contracts — giving people a few months' notice/runway if funding can't be renewed. To be discussed with the Foundation.
